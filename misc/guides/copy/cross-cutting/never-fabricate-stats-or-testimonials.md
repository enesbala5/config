# Never fabricate statistics or testimonials

**Feeds stage:** verify

Choose honest over sensational. Invented numbers and made-up quotes erode trust
and create legal liability; use only proof that actually exists. Treat any
statistic or testimonial in a draft as a claim to verify before it ships.

Treat unearned superlatives and vague proof the same way: "industry-leading"
(according to whom?), "trusted by thousands" (which thousands?), "customers
love us" (show them saying it). If a source can't be named, soften the claim or
cut it rather than dressing it up as evidence.

The same rule covers borrowed reporting: a paraphrase presented as a news
finding is not a source. Tie the claim to something the search actually
returned — an outlet and a date the reader can check — and if it cannot be tied
to a real result, ship the honest option without it instead of inventing the
item. Never promise guaranteed outcomes ("ATS-proof", guaranteed interviews)
either; unearned certainty is fabrication of a different kind.

In comment replies, every concrete claim must trace to the voice/profile files
or the product summary just read. No invented metrics, clients or wins; the
self-check asks whether each concrete claim is traceable to a real source. A
reply is no place to round a number up or claim a result that was never shipped.

The same rule governs technical how-to posts: never invent the walkthrough. Do
not fabricate configs, keybinds, paths or "typical" defaults to fill a gap —
write the setup the author actually runs. If a referenced repo is in play, mine
its history for receipts first; if receipts are still missing, outline the
skeleton and wait rather than filling it with plausible detail.
