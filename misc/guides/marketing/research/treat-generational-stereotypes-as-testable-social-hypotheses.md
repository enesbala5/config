# Treat generational stereotypes as testable social hypotheses

**Feeds stage:** research

Use generational stereotypes to generate specific hypotheses about what people
watch, whom they trust, and why they buy. Inspect the feeds and behavior of the
audience you actually want to reach. Don't assume they prefer creators who look
or sound like them, or that age matters more than platform, product, or life
stage.

Starting hypotheses, not audience facts:

- **Gen Z:** Try native-looking creator clips or memes against polished brand ads.
  A separate hypothesis: small discretionary purchases can hold up even when
  larger purchases feel out of reach. Check actual buying behavior rather
  than assuming either low demand or impulsive spending.
- **Millennials:** Try a problem–solution demo that proves a familiar product is
  better against a lifestyle pitch.
- **Gen X:** Try a peer-aged presenter and a caregiving use case as separate
  hypotheses: who delivers the message and whom the purchase is for.
- **Boomers:** Audit what competes for attention in their actual Facebook or
  Instagram feeds before choosing an opening. If sensational clips or fake
  experts dominate, compete with a stronger honest opener rather than copying
  deceptive tactics.

Write down the expected behavior and what would disprove it before using the
hypothesis in a brief. Judge it on the intended outcome, not age labels or
views alone.
