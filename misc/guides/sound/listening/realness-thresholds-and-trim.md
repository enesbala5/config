# Judge realness on the trim you will use

**Feeds stage:** listening

`earshot realness winner.ogg --start 1.12 --end 1.48` (or a whole folder) judges the trim, not the file: a multi-take file's p_real is a mean over every take and the silence between them. When the user wants real or professionally produced sound, do not use a 'likely code-synthesized' trim, and read the evidence of an 'uncertain' one before using it (write why you kept it). When sourcing a real-recordings soundtrack, gate every downloaded file with `realness` on the exact trim used and record the result in its manifest: an 'uncertain' verdict is usable only with its evidence and a trim-level re-check written there. When synthesized sound is the point (procedural music, UI tones, forge sounds), realness is information, not a gate. Thresholds depend on length and each summary names them: a sound up to 6 s uses the clip thresholds (lo 0.17 / hi 0.77), a longer file the mean of its 4 s windows with weakly calibrated file thresholds (lo 0.40 / hi 0.77) — compare verdicts, not raw p_real, across lengths. Realness hears how a sound sounds, not where it came from: clean professionally synthesized tones (UI sounds, chimes, synth stabs) also read code-synthesized. If the model is missing, say plainly that real-vs-synthetic was not checked. Exact digital zeros prove nothing (most real recordings have edit or fade padding). Held-out accuracy is ROC-AUC 0.92 with whole sound families held out; firm verdicts call 6.7 % of real clips synthesized and 5.5 % of synthesized clips real.
