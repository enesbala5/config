# Phrase the thing first, then steer with adjectives

**Feeds stage:** making-sounds

Name the thing, sound or job first, then feelings, material, numbers: "a footstep on gravel", "a wooden drawer closing", "coin pickup, 8-bit", "waves on a beach, 30 seconds". The moment comes after "when", "as" or "each time": "a soft low thump when the pricing card settles" (thump/soft). Put first, the words of the moment are taken for the sound. "on"/"in" names the surface or place ("rain on wood" is `rain/wood`; an unknown surface gets the nearest and says so: "rain on a tarp" → `no rain on a tarp: rain/tent is the nearest surface`). Product or place words choose nothing ("in my running app, a success sound"). A learned thing answers only when its thing is named: "a heavy impact" gets a designed film hit, "a dumbbell dropped" the recording. To adjust a pick, ask again with adjectives — softer, darker, bigger, longer, drier, not harsh — rather than browsing. Only when the pick is wrong and `also` does not help, use `earshot sounds --search WORDS` (top 20) or `earshot sounds --family step`, then `earshot make "WORDS" --sound ID`. Never list the whole catalogue. Why the thing must be named: on five development sets the first pick is right for 91.8-96.8 % of requests and in the top three for 95.9-99.1 %; on 220 held-out requests written by someone who never saw the catalogue it falls to 69.1 % / 76.4 %. Requests that name the thing or job do far better than mood descriptions. When a learned design and an invented one answer the same request the learned one wins; of the 34 such pairs (`knock/door` and `door/knock`), the invented one stays available by its id. Options win over the words: `--pitch`, `--dur`, `--voice`, `--events`, `--lufs`, the axes `--bright`...`--motion` (-1 to 1) and `--sound ID`.
