# Licence, provenance and attribution are not audio questions

**Feeds stage:** tooling-and-providers

earshot cannot check licence, provenance or attribution — say so when they matter; those questions need a separate source of truth. Everything earshot renders is free to use, commercially too, without credit. The bank's provenance is stated plainly: forge sounds made in code, some tuned toward licensed reference clips and checked to stay originals (CLAP 0.84 at most), plus 15 CC0 recordings fitted as code, each with its source. For recorded sound use real-sound; for a sound written as code and refined against real references use forge (every sample computed, real recordings only references). The code is MIT; everything rendered (`make`, `sfx`, `kit`, `forge`) belongs to whoever rendered it, free for any use, commercial too, without credit. Learned sounds, beds, drums and instrument notes come from public-domain recordings (BigSoundBank, Kenney, VCSL, VSCO-2 CE, Karoryfer and CC0 Freesound uploads), credited in `data/resyn/CREDITS.md`, `data/textures/CREDITS.md`, `data/drums/CREDITS.md` and `data/notes/CREDITS.md` — CC0 asks for no credit and the files keep it anyway. The bank's fitted sounds come from CC0 recordings; a fit you make of your own recording carries that recording's licence. The models come from their authors under their own licences: CLAP Apache-2.0, AST BSD-3-Clause.
