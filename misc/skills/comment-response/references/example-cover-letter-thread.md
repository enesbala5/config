# Worked example: cover letter dilemma thread

This is the reference draft. Everything else in this skill exists to produce something shaped like it.

Input was pasted text: the OP cannot decide what is worse, writing a cover letter for a job that never needed one, or waiting until the application actually asks for one and risking a timeout.

## The draft, as written

> Nowadays everyone asks for it, so unfortunately you need to have one 99% of the time. I didn't want to deal with the same trouble so I ended up spending 2 years building something that should solve the tedious part of preparing a letter & getting it ready to send. I expect to launch it the next few days, if you're interested to get early-access I'd love to send you a link to try it out

Target type: `promote-coverlttr` with an early-access offer. 388 characters, three sentences, no dashes, no link, no product name.

## The three moves

1. **Settle the dilemma they wrote.** A verdict they can act on, in his flat register: everyone asks for it, have one. Not a compliment, not their post summarized back at them. He answers the question they could not answer.
2. **The build line, personal and unnamed.** Their pain first, then his own reason for building, in the same breath: he did not want to deal with it either, so he spent two years on the tedious part. No product name yet, no feature list, no stack.
3. **The offer, with a reason to exist.** A date gives the offer a place to stand ("I expect to launch it the next few days"), then the offer itself is one sentence that asks nothing of them: if they want early access, he will send a link. The link is sent, never pasted.

## What to keep from it

- **Length is not the enemy, padding is.** Three sentences at 388 characters. This is what the drafted comment looks like in practice, so do not compress it into something thinner than the thread deserves.
- **Blunt market verdicts stay blunt.** "99% of the time" beats "in my experience, many employers request one". Do not soften a claim he would make flat out.
- **His own punctuation is his.** The `&` and the comma splices stay. "I expect to launch it the next few days" keeps his grammar shape, it does not get polished into "I expect to launch it in the next few days".
- **Normal capitalization, no formatting.** Sentences start with capitals, as in the draft. No headers, no bold, no bullets in a comment.
- **The offer is allowed on a job search thread.** They are applying right now and the pain is the exact one he built for, which is the condition that makes early access relevant instead of pushy. Off a thread like this, the offer is noise.

## The line not to cross

Same thread, wrong version: naming Coverlttr in the first line, listing tailoring and LaTeX export, or dropping the URL. That reads as an ad and the reply is spent. Peer and peer-adjacent threads get the unnamed build line, never the pitch.

## Two things that go stale

- **Pre-launch framing.** Once it is live, "I expect to launch it the next few days" becomes a false claim. Switch to the live wording instead of reusing this sentence.
- **Recency.** A cover letter dilemma post from six months ago earns nothing. Reply into threads that are hours or days old.
